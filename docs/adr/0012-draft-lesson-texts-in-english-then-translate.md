# Draft Lesson Texts in English, then translate them

A Lesson's English Lesson Text is its original. It is written in its own words
from the Lesson's Sources, and the Thai Lesson Text is translated from it; where
they disagree, the English one is right. The admin reviews every Lesson Text and
can check Thai against English, but cannot read the Danish Sources well enough
to check anything against them. The original has to be the
text the reviewer corrects, so that a correction flows down into the
translations instead of leaving the original wrong. Writing in its own words,
rather than translating the official study material, keeps a Lesson from
reproducing SIRI's text, which states no permission for reuse; a translation of
it would still reproduce it.

## Consequences

- A translation adds nothing of its own. An explanation the learner needs is a
  note in the English Lesson Text, translated with the rest. A Thai-only
  explanation, such as a comparison with Thailand, would be a deliberate,
  recorded exception.
- No Danish Lesson Text is written for now. Danish exam terms reach the learner
  in brackets in the Thai text, kept consistent by a shared term list. A Danish
  Lesson Text, if wanted later, is another translation of the English.
- Because the admin cannot check a draft against its Danish Sources, drafting
  has two stages. A list of bare facts, each citing its page, is taken from the
  Sources and checked against them by an agent that did not write it; the
  English Lesson Text is then written from that list alone, without the
  Sources' text, so it cannot reproduce their wording. The admin checks that
  each line of the list is a bare fact, and that the English reads right; no
  one checks the facts against the Sources but an agent.
- The copyright position rests on the material's colophon and the wording of
  the Copyright Act, not on legal advice or SIRI's answer. That is accepted
  while the app has one invited learner. Before it reaches anyone else, such as
  through an app store or a shared link, get legal advice or SIRI's written
  permission.

## Considered Options

- **Danish original**, with English and Thai translated from it: rejected. It
  is closest to the exam's language, but corrections the admin makes in the
  English would leave the Danish original wrong, and the admin cannot correct
  the Danish confidently.
- **Draft the English straight from the Sources**: rejected. The admin could
  not tell whether a draft was its own words or a close translation of the
  Danish.
- **Danish original, Thai translated directly from it**: rejected. The admin
  cannot verify Danish-to-Thai.
- **Translate the study material word for word**: rejected. Its colophon gives
  no permission to reproduce or translate it.
- **Ask SIRI for permission to translate**: not pursued. Lessons in their own
  words do not depend on an answer, and suit short audio lessons better than a
  246-page textbook read aloud.
